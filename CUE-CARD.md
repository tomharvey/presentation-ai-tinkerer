# Cue card

Rebuilt after five run-throughs, including both sessions with Alex. Every line
is either a phrase to land or a bridge into the next slide — bridges are the
`>` lines, and they are where the talk has been falling apart, not the content.

**It no longer fits one screen.** Bridges cost about thirty lines. Each slide
block does fit, which is what matters live: you only ever need the one you're
on. One thumb-scroll around slide 5. If you want one screen back, delete the
`>` lines and you have the old card.

Nothing is wider than 36 characters. Full script: [[2026-09-22-valencia-script]].

## How to read it

**CAPS are the sentences to land.** Everything lowercase is a reminder of what
the slide is doing. There are eleven capitalised lines; if you hit only those,
the talk still works.

**`>` lines are bridges.** They are the last thing you say on a slide, not the
first thing on the next one — say the bridge, then press the arrow, then pause
while they read. Alex's note, and the single most useful thing anyone has told
you today: *"pause between slides, let people look at the slide first."*

## Why each bridge is what it is

**1 > 2.** The open ends on "everyone wants AI doing more of their job" because
that is the universal hook you found in the second Alex run. It converts a
product-and-engineering story into everyone's problem before anyone can tune
out.

**2 > 3.** "We stopped caring what, we tell it why" is the pivot the whole talk
turns on, and it has to arrive as a decision you made, not a slogan.

**3 > 4.** "None of it works without trust" is the spine. Say it before the four
pillars or they are just a list of things you did.

**4 > 5.** "It does things I didn't want" is what makes auditability necessary
rather than virtuous — you are setting up the problem before offering the fix.

**5 > 6.** "Knowing why doesn't stop it" is the honest limit of the audit trail,
and it is why guardrails exist. Without this bridge, slides 5 and 6 look like
two unrelated good ideas.

**6 > 7.** "So why am I still sat in the middle of it?" turns the pillars into a
question about you, which is where the second half of the talk lives.

**7 > 8.** "Sounds like I automated my job" is the objection, said out loud
before the room says it silently. You landed this one in practice 2 and it has
held since.

**8 > 9.** "Which made why matter MORE" is the loop closing — back to slide 2,
and it earns the closing question rather than just arriving at it.

## The four fixes from the Alex sessions

**"FINDS AND FIXES — not 'tells me'"** is on slide 3 because of Alex's sharpest
catch: you kept saying the AI *tells you* what it found, which puts you back
inside the loop you spend the talk escaping. Watch that verb.

**"I MADE A CONTROVERSIAL CALL"** is the one place Alex's "be more I, less we"
note applies. Everywhere else "we" is doing real work — the talk argues a whole
team changed how it works. But this was a contested judgement and owning it
reads as authority, not a boast.

**"£110m"** — you have said "a hundred and ten million" with no currency, and
euros twice. It is pounds. It also now arrives as the *consequence* of the two
secret sauces rather than as a credential, which is why it finally fits.

**"no email tool"** is Alex's example and it is better than the abstract version:
the third rung isn't "please don't email them", it's that there is no email tool
to reach for.

## Rules, not cues

- Don't read the targets on slide 2 — illustration, not a list.
- Don't say "ontology".
- "59% of those who use it", never "of our customers".
- Don't gate the hiring line on "if you do" — it excludes the people who just
  realised they don't know their why, who are the ones you want.
- Sanity-check "25 people in product and tech".
